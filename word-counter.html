Frequently Asked Questions

How does this word counter work?
The counter analyzes your text in real time as you type or paste. Words are counted by splitting on whitespace, characters include all typed symbols, sentences are detected by punctuation (. ! ?), and paragraphs by double line breaks. Reading time is estimated at 200 words per minute (average adult reader).
Does it count characters with or without spaces?
Both. The "Characters" stat includes spaces and all punctuation. The "No Spaces" stat counts only non-space characters, which is useful for platforms like Twitter or character-limited fields.
How is reading time calculated?
Reading time is estimated at 200 words per minute, which is the average reading speed for an adult. Speaking time uses 130 words per minute, which is the average speaking pace. These are estimates — your actual speed may vary.
Is my text saved anywhere?
No. Everything happens entirely in your browser. Your text is never sent to any server or stored anywhere. When you close the tab, your text is gone.
What are common essay word counts?
A standard paragraph is 100–200 words. A short essay is 500–800 words. A typical college essay is 500–650 words. A 5-paragraph essay is around 500–1000 words. A research paper is usually 1500–5000 words depending on the assignment.
How many pages is 1,000 words?
About 4 pages double-spaced or 2 pages single-spaced in 12pt Times New Roman with 1-inch margins. Handwritten it is roughly 4–5 pages. The full conversion table is further down this page.
How many words is a 5-minute speech?
Around 650 words at a normal presenting pace of 130 words per minute. Write 600–700 words, then rehearse with a timer: nerves make almost everyone speak faster than they planned, so cutting content beats speeding up.
Does the word count include the title and references?
It depends on the brief. Most universities exclude the title page, reference list and appendices, and include the body text plus in-text citations. Paste only the sections that count so your number matches your marker's.
How strict is a 10% word count tolerance?
Where a tolerance exists it is usually ±10%, so a 2,000-word essay is safe between 1,800 and 2,200. Some departments apply a penalty from the first word over the limit instead. Check the module handbook — it is one of the cheapest marks you will ever protect.

Two things shift these numbers. How long is my speech or presentation?

Speaking pace sits between 120 and 150 words per minute for clear delivery; this tool uses 130. That gives a reliable set of anchors: a 1-minute introduction is about 130 words, a 3-minute pitch about 400, a 5-minute class presentation about 650, a 10-minute seminar talk about 1,300, and a 20-minute conference paper about 2,600. Subtract time for slides, questions and pauses — a genuinely good ten-minute talk is usually nearer 1,100 words with room to breathe.

Typical length by assignment type

  • Paragraph: 100–200 words — one idea, evidence, and a link to the next point.
  • Common App / personal statement: 500–650 words, and the limit is enforced.
  • High school essay: 300–1,000 words.
  • Undergraduate essay: 1,500–3,000 words.
  • Literature review: 2,000–6,000 words.
  • Dissertation chapter: 5,000–10,000 words.
  • Abstract: 150–300 words.
  • Blog post that ranks: 1,200–2,000 words.

Common mistakes and how to avoid them

  • Padding to hit the number. "It is important to note that" adds seven words and zero marks. Markers notice filler instantly; add an example or a counter-argument instead.
  • Counting the wrong parts. Including the reference list when the brief excludes it can leave you 300 words short of the real requirement.
  • Trusting an ancient words-per-page rule. "250 words per page" assumes double spacing; single-spaced it is closer to 500. Always check the actual document.
  • Writing to the maximum. Where the limit is 2,000, a tight 1,850 usually scores better than a padded 2,000.
  • Ignoring the character limit. Application portals frequently cap characters, not words, and silently truncate. Use the "no spaces" stat before pasting.
  • Leaving the cut to the end. Trimming 400 words in the final hour destroys structure. Check the count after each section instead.

Advanced tips

Use the target field as a pacing tool. Set your goal, then write in fixed blocks with the Pomodoro timer. Two 25-minute blocks at 300 words each is a finished 600-word draft before lunch, which is far more achievable than "write the essay".

Read the keyword density panel as a repetition check. If one content word appears in 3% or more of your text, you are circling. Vary the phrasing or, more often, vary the idea.

Budget words per section before writing. For a 2,000-word essay: 200 introduction, 1,500 body across three or four arguments, 300 conclusion. Writing to a budget prevents the classic essay that spends 900 words on background and 200 on analysis.

Count as you cut, not after. Paste each finished section here and check whether it earned its length. The paragraph you cannot summarise in one sentence is usually the one to cut.

Do the mechanics separately. Build your reference list with the citation generator and check terminology with the AI tutor so writing time stays writing time.
