How to Make Cornell Notes

The Cornell note-taking system helps you organize, review, and retain information better. Learn the layout, method, and examples in this student guide.

What Is the Cornell Note-Taking System?

Developed at Cornell University, this system divides your page into three sections: a narrow cue column on the left, a wide notes area on the right, and a summary section at the bottom. It's designed to help you review and self-test effectively.

Notes area (right): Record lecture or reading content here. Use abbreviations, bullet points, and leave space between ideas.
Cue column (left): After class, write questions, keywords, or prompts here based on your notes.
Summary (bottom): Write a 2–3 sentence summary of the page in your own words.

How to Make Cornell Notes Step by Step

  1. Divide your page: Draw a vertical line about 2.5 inches from the left edge (cue column), and a horizontal line about 2 inches from the bottom (summary area).
  2. Take notes in the right column: During class, record main ideas, examples, and facts. Use abbreviations and skip full sentences.
  3. Review and create cues: After class, review your notes and write questions or keywords in the left cue column.
  4. Write a summary: At the bottom, summarize the page in 2–3 sentences using your own words.
  5. Study by covering: Cover the notes column, look at your cues, and recite the answers aloud. Then uncover to check.

Why Cornell Notes Work

The Cornell system combines active recall (covering your notes and testing yourself) with spaced repetition (reviewing notes over time). This makes it one of the most evidence-based note-taking methods for students.

Cornell Notes Template and Example

Here is what a filled Cornell page looks like. Imagine a lecture about the water cycle:

Notes area:
Water evaporates from oceans/lakes → rises as vapor → cools and condenses into clouds → falls as precipitation → collects in bodies of water → cycle repeats. Key term: transpiration (water from plants).
Cue column:
What drives evaporation?
Define transpiration
Name 3 precipitation types
Summary: The water cycle moves water between Earth's surface and atmosphere through evaporation, condensation, and precipitation. Plants add water via transpiration.

The cue column holds questions you will later use to test yourself; the summary forces you to compress the page into 2–3 sentences, which is itself a powerful act of comprehension.

Digital vs Paper Cornell Notes

Paper: Slower to write, which forces selectivity; easy to draw diagrams; no distractions. Best for lectures where you need to keep up in real time.

Digital (OneNote, Notion, GoodNotes): Searchable, easy to reorganize, can embed images and audio. Best for textbook chapters and revision. The risk is copying too much because typing is fast.

Whichever you choose, keep the three-section layout. The layout is the method — without the cue column and summary, you just have regular notes.

Common Cornell Notes Mistakes

  • Writing full sentences in the notes column. Use abbreviations and fragments; you are building a map, not a transcript.
  • Skipping the cue column. This removes the self-testing benefit, which is the whole point.
  • Writing a one-word summary. The summary should be 2–3 complete sentences in your own words.
  • Never reviewing. Cornell notes only work if you return to them within 24 hours and again after a few days.
  • Over-decorating. Color-coding every line wastes time. Use color only for genuine emphasis.

A Cornell Notes Study Workflow

  1. During class: Take notes in the right column only. Leave space.
  2. Within 24 hours: Fill in the cue column with questions and keywords.
  3. Same session: Write the 2–3 sentence summary at the bottom.
  4. Before the next class: Cover the notes column and answer the cue questions aloud.
  5. Weekly: Review all summaries and turn tricky cues into flashcards.

Pair this with spaced repetition and active recall for the strongest retention.

When to Use Cornell Notes (and When Not To)

Great for: lectures, textbook chapters, reading assignments, meeting notes, and any content you need to review later.

Less useful for: pure problem-solving (math proofs), step-by-step procedures, and brainstorming sessions. For those, use a dedicated problem log or mind map instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

How wide should the cue column be?

About 2.5 inches (6 cm) on a standard page, roughly one-third of the width. The notes area takes the remaining two-thirds, and the summary strip at the bottom is about 2 inches (5 cm) tall.

Can I use Cornell notes for math?

Yes, but adapt it: use the notes area for worked examples, the cue column for the rule or formula being applied, and the summary for when to use that method. See our how to study math effectively guide.

How long should a Cornell notes session take?

Writing notes during a 50-minute lecture takes the lecture itself. Filling in cues and the summary takes 10–15 minutes after class. Review sessions are 5–10 minutes per page.
