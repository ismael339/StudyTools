What Does a C+ GPA Mean?

A C+ GPA equals 2.3 on a 4.0 scale. Here's what that means for college admissions, scholarships, and how you can raise it.

What Is a C+ GPA?

A C+ GPA is equivalent to a 2.3 on a 4.0 scale. On most high school and college grading scales, a C+ represents a grade between 77% and 79%. It's a passing grade, but it sits below the average of many colleges and universities.

Is a C+ GPA Good or Bad?

Whether a C+ GPA is "good" depends on your goals:

  • For high school graduation: A C+ is typically passing in most US high schools.
  • For community college: A 2.3 GPA is often sufficient for admission.
  • For competitive 4-year colleges: A 2.3 GPA is below the typical admission range (many look for 3.0+).
  • For scholarships: Most merit scholarships require 3.0 or higher.

Can You Get Into College With a C+ GPA?

Yes — but your options may be more limited. Community colleges and some state universities accept students with a 2.3 GPA, especially if you show an upward trend in your grades. Strong SAT/ACT scores, extracurriculars, and a good personal essay can help offset a lower GPA.

The 4.0 GPA Scale Explained

US schools convert letter grades to a 4.0 scale so GPAs can be compared across students and schools. A C+ is worth 2.3 points per credit hour.

  • A (4.0): 90–100% — excellent
  • B (3.0): 80–89% — good
  • C (2.0): 70–79% — average
  • C+ (2.3): 77–79% — slightly above average within the C band
  • D (1.0): 60–69% — below average, often still passing
  • F (0.0): below 60% — failing

Some schools use a weighted scale (4.5 or 5.0) for honors and AP courses, which can lift a C+ student's GPA if they take advanced classes.

How Colleges View a C+ GPA

Admissions officers rarely look at a single number in isolation. They also consider:

  • Trend: A student who earned C+ in 9th grade but A's in 11th and 12th grade is viewed very differently from one whose grades fell.
  • Rigor: A C+ in AP or IB courses can be worth more than an A in easy electives.
  • Context: Schools consider your high school's grading practices and available courses.
  • Test scores: Strong SAT/ACT scores can offset a lower GPA.
  • Essays and recommendations: A compelling personal statement and strong teacher letters can make a real difference.

How GPA Is Calculated

Your GPA is a weighted average of your course grades, where each course's grade points are multiplied by its credit hours, summed, and divided by total credit hours.

Example: If you earn a C+ (2.3) in a 3-credit course and a B (3.0) in a 4-credit course, your GPA = (2.3×3 + 3.0×4) / 7 = 18.9 / 7 = 2.7.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a C+ a passing grade?

Yes. A C+ (2.3) is passing in virtually all US high schools and colleges. However, some programs (nursing, engineering, education) require a minimum C or C+ in prerequisite courses, and a few require a B- or higher.

Can you get scholarships with a C+ GPA?

Merit scholarships usually require 3.0 or higher, but need-based aid, community college scholarships, and some private scholarships do not have GPA minimums. Check with your school's financial aid office.

Does a C+ GPA ruin your chances at graduate school?

Graduate programs typically look for 3.0+, but a strong upward trend, high GRE/GMAT scores, work experience, and strong recommendations can compensate. Many students with a 2.3 undergraduate GPA later succeed in graduate programs.

How many A's do I need to raise a 2.3 to a 3.0?

It depends on how many credits you have completed. With 30 credits at 2.3, you need roughly 15 additional credits at 3.7+ to reach 3.0 overall. Use the GPA calculator with your actual transcript.
